The commitment is not the random value. It’s a hash: H = sha256(seed || salt). You can’t reverse that. So when you commit, nobody (including you, if you’re a third party) can see seed or salt; they only see H. The “randomness” is the revealed (seed, salt) plus the fact that the contract verifies:

sha256(seed || salt) matches the stored commitment.
The BLS signature over that hash is valid for the stored public key.

So before reveal, the outcome is unpredictable (hash hides the inputs). After reveal, it’s verifiable (everyone can check the hash and the signature). The randomness you use in your app is either that revealed value or something derived from it. If you haven't please take a look at thederive_random function that helps you generate u64 randomness (you pass some context & the revealed value).

The flow you're expecting is a bit rudimentary. I'll explain the flow of NebulaVRF with an example of a random DAO vote

Commit (before the draw)
Your backend (or a trusted “VRF operator”) has a keypair and chooses seed and salt. It computes commitment = sha256(seed || salt) and signs the commitment with the BLS key. It then calls the contract: commit(your_address, commitment, pubkey).
Onchain, users only see the commitment and pubkey; they cannot infer seed or salt.

Reveal (after a deadline / when you’re ready)
The same backend calls:
reveal(your_address, seed, salt, signature).
The contract checks that sha256(seed || salt) matches the stored commitment and that the signature is valid. If so, it accepts and the “randomness” for that round is fixed.

Use the randomness in your app
Your app (or any contract) calls:
derive_random(that_verified_randomness, "dao:winner:proposal_42")
and gets a deterministic u64 (e.g. winner index). You can call it again with different contexts (e.g. "dao:winner:proposal_43") to get different numbers from the same revealed randomness.

So: your app (or a designated backend) is the “external actor” that commits and later reveals. The contract does not create the underlying entropy (seed/salt); it only verifies that the revealed (seed, salt, signature) matches the commitment and the pubkey. Once verified, it exposes that value and lets anyone call derive_random to get a  u64 for their context; that’s the “random” number your app uses. The goal of NebulaVRF is not just to generate a random number, but moreso is to ensure verifiability of the random number by using the essence of the commit-reveal scheme

I think the key distinction is about what properties are actually being enforced on-chain. NebulaVRF currently verifies binding and authenticity: once a commitment is posted it cannot be changed, the revealed (seed, salt) must match that commitment, and the reveal is authenticated via a BLS signature tied to a known public key. That gives public verifiability and deterministic derivation, but it does not prove that the entropy itself was generated in an unbiasable or trustless way. We’re not claiming ECVRF-style on-chain proof verification today; instead, NebulaVRF provides verifiable commit–reveal infrastructure with cryptographic authentication, with a roadmap toward stronger entropy guarantees via oracle or multi-party mechanisms.
